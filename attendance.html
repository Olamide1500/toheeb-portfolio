01 · The problem

Every day, the same three questions.

In my workspace, the creative director walked from desk to desk asking: “When did you get in?”, “Have you marked attendance?” and “Are you done for today?”. There was an attendance tool, but it was unclear and stressful to use, so the team went back to a paper checklist.

How might we

Make checking in so quick and clear that people actually use it, and managers can see who’s in without asking around?

02 · Where it started

The tool wasn’t long. It just wasn’t clear.

I didn’t run formal interviews. This came from watching how my own team handled attendance every day.

Insight 01

Clear beats short

The old tool didn’t have many steps. People needed obvious ones.

Insight 02

Managers need answers

Who’s in today should be visible without walking around asking.

Insight 03

Mistakes happen

A wrong click or a dropped network shouldn’t need a message to the manager.

Observed in one workspace, so I treat it as a starting point rather than proof for every team.

03 · Key decisions

Three choices that kept it simple.

01

One click to check in

The main action is the biggest thing on the screen. No menus, no extra page. The button shows progress and ignores repeat clicks, so nobody checks in twice.

Answers: clear beats short
02

Undo for 2 minutes

People click by mistake. Instead of messaging a manager, they can undo it themselves. Undo replaces a confirm dialog, so the normal path stays one click.

Answers: mistakes happen
03

Failures are never silent

If the network drops, the card says so and offers Try again. The status doesn’t change until the server confirms, so there’s never a false check-in.

Answers: you always know if it worked

04 · The hardest part

A calendar that stays calm.

One month shows a lot at once: dates, weekends, check-ins, check-outs, absences, today and the days still to come. Weekends and future days are muted, each day shows one clear status, and clicking a day opens its exact times in a side panel. A list with date filters covers longer ranges.

05 · Visual design

One brand colour, many shades.

Teal carries the brand: buttons, links, the sidebar and focus. Clay is a small accent, never used for actions. Each status pairs a light background with dark text from the same family, so it reads at a glance.

08 · What I learned

What this project taught me.

Simple is hard

The calendar took more hours than any other screen. Making something look calm means deciding what to quiet down.

Clear beats short

People don’t need fewer steps as much as they need obvious ones.

Design for the bad days

Wrong passwords, failed check-ins, expired invites. That’s where people lose trust, so that’s where I spent the most care.

What I’d do differently

Add a gentle reminder on workdays for people who forget to check in when they arrive.

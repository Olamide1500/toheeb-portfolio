01 · The problem

Saving for something real, with money that never arrives on time.

Students save for things that matter: a laptop, school fees, a course. But their money comes from parents, allowances, part-time work, side hustles and gifts, at unpredictable times. When something urgent comes up, the savings plan is the first thing to break.

How might we

Help students save whenever money comes in, keep goal money apart from spending money, and know exactly when they can take it out?

Research summary
Insight 01

Income is irregular

Money arrives at unpredictable times, so fixed schedules don’t fit.

Insight 02

Goals are real

They save for something specific, not just “saving”.

Insight 03

Mental tracking fails

Goal money and spending money blur when it’s all in your head.

A small sample, so I treat it as direction rather than proof for every student.

03 · Key decisions

Three choices that keep the money honest.

01

Rules before money

The lock rule is explained and acknowledged before a goal is created, then shown again on goal details and in support. No surprises after money goes in.

Answers: no surprises
02

An emergency fund that never locks

Its own tab, its own balance and its own rules, so a surprise expense comes out of here instead of breaking a goal.

Answers: irregular income
03

Only “added” when it really is

Money counts as saved only after the payment provider confirms it. Pending is shown separately, and failed payments say so.

Answers: trust

04 · What I left out

Auto-save waits.

Fixed schedules clash with irregular student income: debits fail, or money gets locked at the wrong moment. I also kept out investments, budgeting, streaks, badges and leaderboards, so the MVP does one loop well: add money, keep it safe, take it out on time.

05 · Visual design

Clean indigo, one warm ember.

Neutrals cover about 90% of a screen, indigo about 8% for trust and action, and ember about 2%, used only for growth. Status colours are fixed, so pending, success and failed always look the same.

08 · What I learned

What this project taught me.

Less is a feature

Leaving out auto-save and gamification made the main loop clearer, not weaker.

Write the rules first

Settling every “why” in a decisions log before drawing kept the screens consistent.

Status is trust

With money, “pending” and “added” must never look alike.

What’s left

Finish hi-fi for every journey, build the prototype and confirm the payment partner.

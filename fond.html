01 · The problem

Dating apps show you what someone looks like in a second, but what they want only after days of chatting.

People struggle to find partners who share their values, interests and relationship goals. Most apps are built around a photo and a fast swipe, so people burn out on matches that were never going anywhere.

How might we

Help people who want something serious find others who share what matters to them, without making dating feel like a second job?

02 · Research

People aren’t short of matches. They’re short of the right ones.

Desk research, not interviews: published surveys on how people feel about dating apps, then brainstorming and pressure-testing ideas.

Insight 01

Make intent visible early

Burnout comes from finding out late that you wanted different things.

Insight 02

Less volume, more fit

Too many matches overwhelms some people and leaves others feeling ignored.

Insight 03

Safety is part of the product

Control over who sees you shouldn’t be buried in settings.

Surveys are US-based, so I treat them as a signal rather than proof for every market.

03 · Key decisions

Three choices that shape how FOND feels.

01

Pass or Interested, not a swipe

Two labelled buttons make each choice feel quicker and calmer. You read the card, then give one clear answer. “Interested” says you’d like to talk, not that you’re rating someone.

Answers: less volume, more fit
02

Relationship goals up front

Sign-up asks “What are you looking for?” with honest options: Long-term partner, Open to see, or Not sure yet. The same choice appears in Filters, so intent is clear before anyone says hello.

Answers: make intent visible early
03

Control over who sees you

People choose who can see their profile: everyone, matches only, people they’ve liked, or no one. Blocked users and active devices live in the same place.

Answers: safety is part of the product

04 · Feature spotlight

Compatibility you can actually read.

A single number isn’t enough to trust. The match profile shows the score, and one tap opens the full breakdown: relationship goals, shared interests, lifestyle, and age and location. It ends with a ready-made first question, so nobody stares at an empty chat.

05 · Visual design

Calm, warm and a little romantic.

Most dating apps shout in bright pink and red. FOND uses a deep wine on soft cream, so it feels more like a quiet coffee date than a night out.

08 · What I learned

What FOND taught me.

Details make it real

The empty states, errors and small settings make an app feel finished.

Words shape feelings

Changing “Like” to “Interested” did more for the tone than any colour choice.

Systems save time

Building components first kept 65 screens consistent and made late changes painless.

People come first

Next time I’ll put a prototype in front of real users before I reach high fidelity.
